01 · Context & Problem

Why First-Day Orientation Fails New Students

First-Day Spatial Confusion

Arriving at NEOMA is exciting yet overwhelming—students struggle to locate the Library, Student Services, Administration, and common study zones.

Outdated & Passive Methods

Traditional onboarding relies on long, passive info lectures and static paper maps that fail to engage modern students.

Missed Vital Information

Students frequently miss out on essential campus resources and academic support simply because they cannot find the right office early on.

Fragmented Integration

High orientation stress creates social barriers, making it harder for first-year and international exchange students to connect.

02 · Our Solution

Road to Uni: Interactive Campus Escape Game

Designed for first-year undergraduate & international exchange students—turning a stressful first day into a collaborative campus adventure.

Location-Based Puzzles

At each campus stop, players solve interactive riddles that require exploring real rooms and discovering practical services.

Social Icebreaker

Encourages newcomers to team up, talk with peers, and feel part of the NEOMA community right from day one.

Active Learning

Replaces passive guidebooks with hands-on exploration so students remember where everything is located.

03 · Vision, Objectives & Scope

Clear Targets & Strict Scope Boundaries

Our Vision: Make university onboarding fun, social, and stress-free from day one through interactive campus discovery.

SMART Success Targets

  • 5 Zones

    Design unique, interactive escape game challenges across 5 key campus locations.

  • ≥ 80%

    Task completion rate achieved during live usability tests with real students.

  • ≥ 80%

    Student satisfaction score validating campus discovery and engagement value.

Scope Boundaries

Included (In Scope)

Escape game storyline, puzzle rules, user flow, interactive UI screen wireframes, and live student usability testing.

Excluded (Out of Scope)

Native iOS/Android software coding, App Store deployment, and personal student data collection (full GDPR compliance).

04 · Risks & Team Challenges

How We Handled Real Project Obstacles

Issue · Week 3 4 → 3 Members

Team Capacity Drop

One member left the course in Week 3. Instead of panicking, we activated our Team Working Agreement and immediately redistributed tasks across the 3 remaining members—keeping every deadline on track.

Technical Risk Mitigated Early

Zero Native Coding Skills

Building a native mobile app without programming expertise risked total failure. We pivoted early to a high-fidelity interactive prototype to validate the experience realistically.

Validation Risk ≥ 4 Testers

Recruiting Student Testers

Finding busy students to test our game was a key risk. We offered snack incentives and ran trials during peak library hours, easily reaching our target sample.

Stakeholder Risk Early Buy-In

Campus Rules & Approvals

Using campus buildings and branding required institutional permission. We consulted NEOMA Administration & BDE/BDI early to guarantee safety and compliance.

05 · Diagnosis & Key Takeaway

Project Diagnosis: What Saved Us

Sticking to an unrealistic ambition—coding a full mobile app with no programming skills—would have caused the project to fail. Re-scoping early and reacting immediately to our Week 3 team size drop kept the project 100% on track.

The Single Most Important Lesson

“Project management is not about following a rigid, perfect plan—it is about dynamic flexibility, fixing problems fast, and always keeping user needs first.”
